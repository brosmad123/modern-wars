THE BIG PICTURE

Why Norway Leads the World

Norway's unique geography — deep fjords, mountain rivers, and vast coastlines — created a natural infrastructure for clean energy centuries before the rest of the world caught up.

🏔️

Geography as Power

Thousands of glacial rivers and steep mountain terrain create the perfect conditions for hydroelectric generation — nature's own turbines.

💧

Hydro Dominance

Hydropower accounts for roughly 92% of Norway's total electricity production — the highest share of any large country on Earth.

🌬️

Growing Wind Fleet

With over 1,700 km of coastline and powerful North Sea winds, Norway is rapidly expanding its onshore and offshore wind capacity.

🚗

EV Revolution

Norway has the highest EV adoption rate per capita in the world. In 2023, over 80% of new car sales were fully electric vehicles.

THE BACKBONE OF NORWAY

Hydroelectric Power

Norway's mountain-carved landscape collects rainfall and snowmelt in thousands of natural and artificial reservoirs, releasing it through tunnels to spin giant turbines deep underground.

ANIMATED HYDROELECTRIC DAM

Water held in mountain reservoirs flows down penstocks, spinning turbines to generate electricity before rejoining the river below.

1,700+
Hydropower Plants in Norway

From massive installations like Kvilldal (1,240 MW) to small mountain turbines, Norway's hydro network is vast.

31,000
MW of Installed Capacity

Norway's total hydropower capacity could power Germany's entire residential sector.

120 TWh
Annual Hydro Production

Norway generates more electricity per person from hydropower than almost any other nation on Earth.

THE NEXT FRONTIER

Wind Energy

With powerful North Sea storms and thousands of kilometers of exposed coastline, Norway is racing to build one of Europe's largest offshore wind portfolios.

Hywind Scotland

Norway's Equinor built the world's first floating offshore wind farm off Scotland's coast in 2017, pioneering technology now used globally.

OFFSHORE PIONEER

Fosen Vind

One of Europe's largest onshore wind farms, located in Trøndelag, with a combined capacity of 1,057 MW — enough to power 170,000 homes.

ONSHORE GIANT

Sørlige Nordsjø II

A massive planned offshore wind project in the North Sea that could generate up to 3,000 MW — Norway's biggest wind project to date.
